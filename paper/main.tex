\title{Geometric Primitives Dominate the Perceptual Frontier\\
of Sub-300-Byte Image Placeholders}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Jianqi Pan}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{(affiliation withheld for review)}\\
jannchie@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Image placeholders---the low-fidelity previews shown while a full image
loads---are ubiquitous in production systems (BlurHash, ThumbHash, SQIP) yet
have never been evaluated in the academic literature, and the sub-300-byte
regime they occupy lies below where standard codecs operate. We present the
first perceptual rate--distortion study of this regime, comparing
geometric-primitive hashing against industrial placeholder formats, a faithful
reimplementation of the closest academic prior (Marwood \textit{et al.}, ICIP
2018), and the smallest attainable JPEG/WebP, across Kodak and CLIC under PSNR,
SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS. Geometric primitives Pareto-dominate on perceptual
metrics: a 20-byte circle hash beats a 166-byte BlurHash on LPIPS, and a
77-byte triangle hash matches the 187-byte Marwood mesh at $2.4\times$ smaller
size while encoding $189\times$ faster than SQIP. Counter-intuitively, the
MSE-optimal triangulation \emph{wins} PSNR but \emph{loses} LPIPS; through three
ablations (joint refinement, perceptual weighting, entropy coding) we show the
binding constraint in this regime is primitive expressiveness, not the search
objective or the serialization.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
image placeholders, ultra-low bitrate, rate--distortion, perceptual quality,

\section{Introduction}
A modern web or mobile client almost never shows a blank box while an image
loads. Instead it renders a \emph{placeholder}: a tiny, instantly-decodable
preview embedded directly in the page payload or the API response. The dominant
production techniques---BlurHash~\cite{blurhash}, ThumbHash~\cite{thumbhash},
and SQIP~\cite{sqip}---compress a thumbnail to \emph{tens of bytes}, small
enough to inline thousands of them in a single response without a second network
round trip.

Two facts motivate this paper. First, despite serving billions of placeholders
daily, \emph{none} of these formats has been evaluated in the peer-reviewed
literature; their only documentation is source repositories and engineering
blogs, with no datasets, no metrics, and no baselines. Second, the byte budget
they target---roughly $20$ to $300$ bytes, i.e. well under $0.01$ bits per
pixel for a $256$-pixel preview---is a regime where conventional codecs simply
do not operate: the smallest valid WebP we could emit lands near $85$--$150$
bytes and the smallest JPEG near $320$, both with header overhead that dwarfs
the payload.

We close this gap with the first systematic, perceptually-grounded
rate--distortion (R--D) study of sub-300-byte placeholders. Our subject is
geometric-primitive hashing---fitting a handful of translucent circles,
triangles, or rectangles to a thumbnail and quantizing their parameters to a
fixed-width bitstream, in the lineage of Primitive~\cite{primitive} and
SQIP~\cite{sqip}. We compare it against the industrial formats above, against a
faithful reimplementation of the closest academic prior
(Marwood~\cite{marwood}), and against codec extremes, on Kodak and CLIC, under
both fidelity (PSNR, SSIM) and perceptual (LPIPS~\cite{lpips},
DISTS~\cite{dists}) metrics. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the first academic R--D benchmark of industrial placeholder formats,
  showing geometric primitives Pareto-dominate on perceptual quality across the
  whole regime (\S\ref{sec:rd});
  \item a head-to-head with a faithful Marwood~\cite{marwood} reimplementation
  exposing a stark PSNR--perception split: the MSE-optimal mesh wins PSNR but is
  beaten $2.4\times$ on LPIPS (\S\ref{sec:marwood});
  \item an encode-latency axis showing primitive hashing is $189\times$ faster
  than the only comparable geometric method (\S\ref{sec:speed}); and
  \item three ablations---joint refinement, perceptual weighting, entropy
  coding---that together localize the regime's bottleneck to primitive
  \emph{expressiveness}, not the objective or the serialization
  (\S\ref{sec:ablation}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Placeholders.} BlurHash~\cite{blurhash} stores a few DCT coefficients in
a base-83 string; ThumbHash~\cite{thumbhash} is a tighter DCT variant with
alpha and aspect; SQIP~\cite{sqip} traces a Primitive~\cite{primitive} fit to an
SVG. All are engineering artifacts without published evaluation.

\textbf{Primitive fitting and vectorization.} Hill-climbing or annealing fits of
geometric primitives to images originate with Primitive~\cite{primitive} and
Geometrize; the differentiable-rendering line (diffvg~\cite{diffvg},
LIVE~\cite{live}) optimizes vector parameters by gradient descent but produces
SVGs far larger than a placeholder and takes seconds to minutes per image.

\textbf{Ultra-low-bitrate coding.} Marwood \textit{et al.}~\cite{marwood}
represent images in $\sim$200 bytes via a Delaunay triangulation with palette
vertex colors and ANS coding---the closest prior to our regime, which we
reimplement in \S\ref{sec:marwood}. GaussianImage~\cite{gaussianimage} fits 2D
Gaussians with quantization-aware fine-tuning, but operates at $0.1$--$1$ bpp,
an order of magnitude above us. Generative coders (PerCo~\cite{perco}) reach
$<0.01$ bpp but require gigabyte-scale diffusion decoders, the opposite of a
model-free byte-addressable placeholder. The rate--distortion-primitive lineage
traces to matching-pursuit coding with anisotropic atoms~\cite{mp}.

\textbf{Perceptual metrics.} We adopt LPIPS~\cite{lpips} and DISTS~\cite{dists}
as primary metrics; both are learned full-reference measures that correlate with
human judgment far better than PSNR/SSIM at low fidelity.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
We study \texttt{arthash}, an open primitive-hashing codec. Given an image it
builds a thumbnail and a mean-color background, then greedily fits
$N$ primitives. Each primitive is placed by a two-stage search: (i) a
residual-weighted random initialization, and (ii) a Gaussian hill-climb on the
geometry with the blend $\alpha$ held fixed, evaluated against the current
residual. Per-pixel error of a candidate is the change in sum-of-squared-error
(SSE) it induces when alpha-composited onto the canvas; this is evaluated in
$O(1)$ (axis-aligned shapes) or $O(\text{bbox height})$ (circle/triangle) via
integral images, so a full $12$-shape fit costs $\sim$1\,ms. After the geometry
is fixed, a sweep over quantized $\alpha$ levels picks the best blend, and the
chosen color and geometry are quantized to a fixed-width bitstream (e.g.\ a
circle is $5{+}5$ position, $4$ radius, $16$ color, $3$ alpha bits).

\textbf{Quantization-aware joint refinement.} The greedy fit never revisits a
placed primitive. We add an optional backfitting pass: each primitive is
removed, the canvas re-rendered, a fresh primitive re-searched against it, and
the replacement kept only if it lowers the \emph{exact total} SSE. Crucially the
accept test renders \emph{wire-quantized} parameters, so it judges what the
decoder will actually produce---a continuous-domain win can otherwise flip sign
under quantization. This is byte-format-preserving (default off) and analyzed in
\S\ref{sec:ablation}.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
All methods encode from a $100$-pixel long-edge LANCZOS thumbnail, decode and
upscale to a $256$-pixel long-edge canvas, and are scored against the $256$-pixel
LANCZOS ground truth. Corpora are Kodak ($24$) and CLIC2020 professional-valid
($41$). Metrics: bytes, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (AlexNet), DISTS. Baselines: BlurHash
(component sweep), ThumbHash, \texttt{arthash} DCT and shape modes
(circle/triangle/rect/square, shape-count sweep), and the smallest JPEG/WebP the
encoders emit. The \texttt{arthash} variants share one codec; shape modes differ
only in the rasterized primitive.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Primitives dominate the perceptual frontier}\label{sec:rd}
Table~\ref{tab:kodak} and Fig.~\ref{fig:rd} report the Kodak R--D frontier. The
geometric shape modes Pareto-dominate every baseline on \emph{both} perceptual
metrics across the entire $20$--$300$-byte regime, without trading away PSNR.
The most striking point reproduces on CLIC: a $20$-byte circle hash
(LPIPS $0.70$) beats a $166$-byte BlurHash (LPIPS $0.82$)---an $\sim$8$\times$
byte advantage at equal-or-better perceptual quality. SSIM is the lone metric
that fails to separate methods (all in $0.32$--$0.46$); LPIPS and DISTS agree on
the ordering and carry the argument. Notably, BlurHash scales poorly here
($22{\to}166$\,B buys only LPIPS $0.881{\to}0.867$): spending more DCT
coefficients is the wrong use of bytes---geometric structure is the better
target.

\subsection{Versus the academic baseline (Marwood ICIP'18)}\label{sec:marwood}
The closest academic prior, Marwood~\cite{marwood}, was never released. We
reimplement it faithfully: vertices on a $g{\times}g$ grid with Delaunay
connectivity recomputed at decode (hence free), a $K$-color palette of vertex
indices, Gouraud-interpolated fill, error-driven greedy placement with palette
coordinate descent, and an \emph{ideal-entropy} byte model (a perfect arithmetic
coder---deliberately generous to the baseline). On the paper's own setting
($221$\,px, $\sim$200\,B) our reimplementation reaches the reported magnitude
($\sim$24\,dB on simple content, $16$--$20$\,dB on hard; the paper's ImageNet
mean is $\sim$25\,dB and Kodak is harder), so it is not a strawman.

Fig.~\ref{fig:marwood} is the study's sharpest result. Marwood \emph{wins PSNR}
(its Gouraud mesh is MSE-optimal, $0.5$--$1$\,dB higher at equal bytes) but
\emph{loses LPIPS decisively}: \texttt{arthash} triangle-12 matches Marwood's
\emph{187-byte} LPIPS at \emph{77 bytes} ($2.4\times$ smaller) and is better at
every rate. Gouraud interpolation smooths away precisely the structure LPIPS
rewards---the same failure mode as BlurHash. Optimizing PSNR optimizes the wrong
metric for placeholders.

frontier. Pure encode latency (median over Kodak thumbnails) against mean LPIPS

The honest comparison is against SQIP---the only other primitive-fitting
method, built for quality not speed: \texttt{arthash} triangle-12 encodes in
$1.5$\,ms versus SQIP's $284$\,ms, \textbf{$189\times$ faster}, at $\sim$20$\times$

\subsection{Where the gains are not: three ablations}\label{sec:ablation}
If primitives win, what is the remaining lever---the objective, or the
serialization? Three ablations bound both.

\textbf{Joint refinement} (\S\ref{sec:method}) reliably improves PSNR
($+0.02$ to $+0.20$\,dB) but \emph{not} perception: on Kodak, LPIPS is mixed
(triangle $-1.2\%$, circle/rect $+0.4$--$0.8\%$). Minimizing SSE shrinks or
merges primitives in flat regions to shave error while giving up edge structure
LPIPS rewards.

\textbf{Perceptual weighting.} The obvious fix---minimize
$\sum w\,(t{-}c)^2$ with $w$ emphasizing edges or saliency---fails. With
identical RNG (so only the weight map varies), edge and center weighting make
LPIPS \emph{worse} and the best (strong saliency) buys $-0.5\%$ LPIPS for
$-0.65$\,dB PSNR, unstable per image. Twelve solid circles cannot represent
edges, so steering toward them sacrifices the flat-region coverage LPIPS also
rewards.

\textbf{Entropy coding.} A static corpus-trained model over the fixed-width
fields saves only $3$--$5\%$ cross-dataset ($<10\%$ even in-corpus); the
residual-driven fitter already spreads positions near-uniformly and uses the
full color gamut, so the fields are near-maximum-entropy.

\section{Discussion}
The three ablations converge: neither a perceptual objective nor entropy coding
moves the needle, and the Marwood comparison shows an MSE-optimal mesh is
perceptually \emph{worse} than coarse hard-edged primitives. The binding
constraint in this regime is the primitives' \emph{expressiveness}---what shapes
can represent at all---not how they are searched or serialized. This explains
why triangles, which carry oriented edges, beat circles perceptually throughout,
and why smoothing methods (BlurHash, Gouraud meshes) collapse on LPIPS while
holding PSNR. The actionable corollary for future work is to enrich the
primitive vocabulary (mixed-primitive selection, soft or differentiable edges),
not to refine the loss.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented the first perceptual rate--distortion study of sub-300-byte image
placeholders. Geometric-primitive hashing Pareto-dominates industrial formats
and a faithful Marwood~\cite{marwood} reimplementation on LPIPS and DISTS---by up
to $2.4$--$8\times$ in bytes---while encoding two orders of magnitude faster than
the only comparable geometric method, and we localize the regime's bottleneck to
primitive expressiveness. We hope this establishes placeholders as a measurable
research target rather than folklore.
