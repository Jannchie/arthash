\title{Geometric Primitives Dominate the Perceptual Frontier\\
of Sub-300-Byte Image Placeholders}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Jianqi Pan}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Independent Researcher}\\
jannchie@gmail.com}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Image placeholders---the low-fidelity previews shown while a full image
loads---are ubiquitous in production systems (BlurHash, ThumbHash, SQIP) yet
have never been evaluated in the academic literature, and the sub-300-byte
regime they occupy lies below where standard codecs operate. This is a
\emph{measurement} paper: we present the first perceptual rate--distortion study
of that regime, putting industrial placeholder formats, a faithful
reimplementation of the closest academic prior (Marwood \textit{et al.}, ICIP
2018), and the smallest attainable JPEG/WebP on common ground across Kodak and
CLIC under PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS. The measurement yields one
counter-intuitive finding. Geometric primitives Pareto-dominate the perceptual
frontier---a 20-byte circle hash beats a 166-byte BlurHash on LPIPS---while every
frequency/smoothing representation collapses on it, including the MSE-optimal
Marwood mesh, which \emph{wins} PSNR yet \emph{loses} LPIPS $2.4\times$. In this
regime PSNR is anti-correlated with perceptual quality. Three controls (a
quantization-aware search refinement, perceptual objective weighting, and
entropy coding) each fail to move the perceptual frontier, ruling out the search
objective and the serialization and localizing the binding constraint to
primitive \emph{expressiveness}.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
image placeholders, ultra-low bitrate, rate--distortion, perceptual quality,

\section{Introduction}
A modern web or mobile client almost never shows a blank box while an image
loads. Instead it renders a \emph{placeholder}: a tiny, instantly-decodable
preview embedded directly in the page payload or the API response. The dominant
production techniques---BlurHash~\cite{blurhash}, ThumbHash~\cite{thumbhash},
and SQIP~\cite{sqip}---compress a thumbnail to \emph{tens of bytes}, small
enough to inline thousands of them in a single response without a second network
round trip.

Two facts make this a measurement gap worth closing. First, despite serving
billions of placeholders daily, \emph{none} of these formats has been evaluated
in the peer-reviewed literature; their only documentation is source repositories
and engineering blogs, with no datasets, no metrics, and no baselines. Second,
the byte budget they target---roughly $20$ to $300$ bytes, i.e.\ well under
$0.01$ bits per pixel for a $256$-pixel preview---is a regime where conventional
codecs simply do not operate: the smallest valid WebP we could emit lands near
$85$--$150$ bytes and the smallest JPEG near $320$, both with header overhead
that dwarfs the payload. Nobody has characterized what the rate--distortion
frontier even looks like here, let alone under metrics that track human
perception.

This paper is a measurement study that closes the gap, and the measurement turns
up a result that runs against the default intuition of low-bitrate coding. The
reflex in this setting is to minimize distortion (MSE) and to spend any spare
bits on a better entropy coder. We find that in the sub-300-byte regime that
reflex is wrong on both counts: representations built from \emph{geometric
primitives}---a handful of translucent circles, triangles, or rectangles fitted
to a thumbnail, in the lineage of Primitive~\cite{primitive} and
SQIP~\cite{sqip}---Pareto-dominate the perceptual frontier, while every
frequency-domain or smoothing representation collapses on it even as it holds or
improves PSNR---an ultra-low-bitrate instance of the perception--distortion
tradeoff~\cite{blau2018perception,rdp} that, to our knowledge, has not been
measured at this scale before. Our contributions are three, in the shape of a
measurement paper:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A benchmark.} The first academic perceptual R--D evaluation of
  the sub-300-byte regime, placing industrial placeholder formats, a faithful
  reimplementation of the closest academic prior (Marwood~\cite{marwood}), and
  codec extremes on common ground under both fidelity (PSNR, SSIM) and
  perceptual (LPIPS~\cite{lpips}, DISTS~\cite{dists}) metrics, with a second,
  encode-latency axis (\S\ref{sec:method}, \S\ref{sec:rd}, \S\ref{sec:speed}).
  \item \textbf{A finding.} Geometric primitives Pareto-dominate the perceptual
  frontier while every frequency/smoothing representation collapses on it. We
  show this within a single codec (its own DCT mode, a controlled comparison
  that varies only the representation) and against the MSE-optimal
  Marwood~\cite{marwood} mesh, which \emph{wins} PSNR yet \emph{loses} LPIPS
  $2.4\times$: in this regime PSNR is anti-correlated with perceptual quality
  (\S\ref{sec:rd}, \S\ref{sec:marwood}).
  \item \textbf{A localization.} Three controls---a quantization-aware search
  refinement, perceptual objective weighting, and entropy coding---each fail to
  move the perceptual frontier, ruling out the search objective and the
  serialization and localizing the binding constraint to primitive
  \emph{expressiveness} (\S\ref{sec:ablation}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Placeholders.} BlurHash~\cite{blurhash} stores a few DCT coefficients in
a base-83 string; ThumbHash~\cite{thumbhash} is a tighter DCT variant with
alpha and aspect; SQIP~\cite{sqip} traces a Primitive~\cite{primitive} fit to an
SVG. All are engineering artifacts without published evaluation---precisely the
gap this measurement study fills.

\textbf{Primitive fitting, vectorization, and triangulation coding.}
Hill-climbing or annealing fits of geometric primitives to images originate with
Primitive~\cite{primitive} and Geometrize; the differentiable-rendering line
(diffvg~\cite{diffvg}, LIVE~\cite{live}) optimizes vector parameters by gradient
descent but yields SVGs far larger than a placeholder and takes seconds to
minutes per image. Triangulation-based coding is an older parallel
lineage---linear splines over adaptive triangulations~\cite{demaret2006linearsplines},
data-dependent triangulations~\cite{lehner2007ddt}, and recently differentiable
curved-triangle meshes~\cite{wang2024curvedtri}---all optimizing fidelity
(MSE/PSNR) at ordinary rates; that geometry-adaptive representations can beat
fixed frequency bases is itself a long-standing idea
(bandelets~\cite{lepennec2005bandelets}). Compact \emph{learned} representations
such as COIN~\cite{dupont2021coin} reach tens-of-bytes scale by overfitting a
per-image MLP, and 2D Gaussian splatting (after~\cite{kerbl2023gaussian})
underlies GaussianImage below; both pay a per-image decoder, unlike a
byte-addressable primitive hash.

\textbf{Ultra-low-bitrate and perceptual coding.} Marwood \textit{et al.}~\cite{marwood}
represent images in $\sim$200 bytes via a Delaunay triangulation with palette
vertex colors and ANS coding---the closest prior to our regime, which we
reimplement and contrast in \S\ref{sec:marwood}. GaussianImage~\cite{gaussianimage}
fits 2D Gaussians with quantization-aware fine-tuning, but operates at $0.1$--$1$
bpp, an order of magnitude above us. On the learned side, perceptual codecs
establish that at low rate, optimizing for realism beats minimizing
distortion---HiFiC~\cite{hific} with a GAN decoder, extreme GAN compression that
synthesizes content from semantic maps~\cite{agustsson2019extreme}, and
generative coders (PerCo~\cite{perco}) reaching $<0.01$ bpp---but all require
large generative decoders, the opposite of a model-free byte-addressable
placeholder. The rate--distortion-primitive lineage traces to matching-pursuit
coding with anisotropic atoms~\cite{mp}.

\textbf{Perceptual metrics and the distortion--perception gap.} We adopt
LPIPS~\cite{lpips} and DISTS~\cite{dists} as primary metrics; both are learned
full-reference measures that correlate with human judgment far better than
PSNR/SSIM at low fidelity~\cite{wang2009mse}. That a representation can lower
distortion yet look worse is not anecdotal: the perception--distortion
tradeoff~\cite{blau2018perception} proves the two are in tension, and its
rate-constrained form, the rate--distortion--perception tradeoff~\cite{rdp},
predicts exactly the split we measure. Our study is, to our knowledge, the first
empirical instantiation of that tradeoff at the sub-300-byte, real-image
placeholder scale; the PSNR--perception divergence is here not noise but the
central finding.

\section{Measurement Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Protocol.} Every method encodes from a $100$-pixel long-edge LANCZOS
thumbnail, decodes and upscales to a $256$-pixel long-edge canvas, and is scored
against the $256$-pixel LANCZOS ground truth. Corpora are Kodak ($24$) and
CLIC2020 professional-valid ($41$). Metrics: bytes, PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS (AlexNet),
DISTS.

\textbf{Methods under test.} We span three representational families. (i)
\emph{Frequency/smoothing}: BlurHash (component sweep), ThumbHash, and the
smallest JPEG/WebP the encoders emit. (ii) \emph{Geometric primitives}: an open
primitive-hashing codec, \texttt{arthash}, in its circle / triangle / rect /
square modes (shape-count sweep). (iii) The academic prior, Marwood
(\S\ref{sec:marwood}). Crucially, \texttt{arthash} also ships a \emph{DCT} mode
that shares the same codec, quantizer, and evaluation harness as its geometric
modes and differs only in what it represents; we include it as a
\emph{within-codec control} that isolates the representational paradigm from
implementation details.

\textbf{The geometric codec.} Given an image, \texttt{arthash} builds a
thumbnail and a mean-color background, then greedily fits $N$ primitives. Each
primitive is placed by a two-stage search: (i) a residual-weighted random
initialization, and (ii) a Gaussian hill-climb on the geometry with the blend
$\alpha$ held fixed, evaluated against the current residual. The cost of a
candidate is the change in sum-of-squared-error (SSE) it induces when
alpha-composited onto the canvas; this is evaluated in $O(1)$ (axis-aligned
shapes) or $O(\text{bbox height})$ (circle/triangle) via integral images, so a
full $12$-shape fit costs $\sim$1\,ms. After the geometry is fixed, a sweep over
quantized $\alpha$ levels picks the best blend, and the chosen color and geometry
are quantized to a fixed-width bitstream (e.g.\ a circle is $5{+}5$ position, $4$
radius, $16$ color, $3$ alpha bits). This greedy, MSE-driven fit---the natural
baseline objective---is exactly what \S\ref{sec:ablation} probes for slack.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Primitives dominate the perceptual frontier}\label{sec:rd}
Table~\ref{tab:kodak} and Fig.~\ref{fig:rd} report the Kodak R--D frontier,
grouped by representational family. The geometric modes Pareto-dominate every
frequency/smoothing baseline on \emph{both} perceptual metrics across the entire
$20$--$300$-byte regime, without trading away PSNR. The most striking point
reproduces on CLIC: a $20$-byte circle hash (LPIPS $0.70$) beats a $166$-byte
BlurHash (LPIPS $0.82$)---an $\sim$8$\times$ byte advantage at equal-or-better
perceptual quality.

Two reads of the table sharpen the finding. First, the \emph{within-codec
control}: \texttt{arthash}'s own DCT mode (LPIPS $0.905$) lands squarely in
BlurHash/ThumbHash territory, nowhere near its geometric siblings (LPIPS
$0.63$--$0.70$) despite sharing every line of codec and quantizer. The gap is the
representation, not the implementation. Second, frequency representations
\emph{scale} poorly here: BlurHash spends $22{\to}166$\,B to buy only LPIPS
$0.881{\to}0.867$---more DCT coefficients is the wrong use of bytes. SSIM is the
lone metric that fails to separate methods (all in $0.32$--$0.46$); LPIPS and
DISTS agree on the ordering and carry the argument.

\subsection{The PSNR--perception split: versus Marwood (ICIP'18)}\label{sec:marwood}
The within-codec control shows frequency representations collapse perceptually;
the academic prior shows the collapse is about \emph{smoothing}, not about being
a weak method. Marwood~\cite{marwood} is the strongest MSE-driven baseline in our
regime and was never released, so we reimplement it faithfully: vertices on a
$g{\times}g$ grid with Delaunay connectivity recomputed at decode (hence free), a
$K$-color palette of vertex indices, Gouraud-interpolated fill, error-driven
greedy placement with palette coordinate descent, and an \emph{ideal-entropy}
byte model (a perfect arithmetic coder---deliberately generous to the baseline).
On the paper's own setting ($221$\,px, $\sim$200\,B) our reimplementation reaches
the reported magnitude ($\sim$24\,dB on simple content, $16$--$20$\,dB on hard;
the paper's ImageNet mean is $\sim$25\,dB and Kodak is harder), so it is not a
strawman.

Fig.~\ref{fig:marwood} is the study's sharpest result. Marwood \emph{wins PSNR}
(its Gouraud mesh is MSE-optimal, $0.5$--$1$\,dB higher at equal bytes) but
\emph{loses LPIPS decisively}: \texttt{arthash} triangle-12 matches Marwood's
\emph{187-byte} LPIPS at \emph{77 bytes} ($2.4\times$ smaller) and is better at
every rate. A method engineered and entropy-coded to minimize distortion is
beaten, perceptually, by coarse hard-edged primitives---because Gouraud
interpolation smooths away precisely the structure LPIPS rewards, the same
failure mode as BlurHash. In this regime, optimizing PSNR optimizes the wrong
metric.

\subsection{Localizing the bottleneck: three controls}\label{sec:ablation}
The finding is that primitives win and smoothing loses. The natural next
question is \emph{what the remaining lever is}---a better search objective, or a
better serialization. We run three controls; all three come back negative, and
their convergence is what pins the cause to expressiveness.

\textbf{Control 1: a better search objective (quantization-aware refinement).}
The greedy fit never revisits a placed primitive, so we add a backfitting pass:
each primitive is removed, the canvas re-rendered, a fresh primitive re-searched,
and the replacement kept only if it lowers the \emph{exact total} SSE. The accept
test renders \emph{wire-quantized} parameters, so it judges what the decoder will
actually produce (a continuous-domain win can otherwise flip sign under
quantization); it is byte-format-preserving and off by default. Run as an SSE
optimizer it does exactly its job---PSNR rises everywhere ($+0.02$ to
$+0.20$\,dB)---but perception does not follow: on Kodak LPIPS is mixed (triangle
$-1.2\%$, circle/rect $+0.4$--$0.8\%$). Minimizing SSE shrinks or merges
primitives in flat regions to shave error while giving up the edge structure
LPIPS rewards. A better MSE search does not buy perceptual quality.

\textbf{Control 2: a perceptual objective (weighting).} The obvious fix---minimize
$\sum w\,(t{-}c)^2$ with $w$ emphasizing edges or saliency, which stays
compatible with the fast integral-image evaluation---also fails. With identical
RNG (so only the weight map varies), edge and center weighting make LPIPS
\emph{worse}, and the best (strong saliency) buys $-0.5\%$ LPIPS for $-0.65$\,dB
PSNR, unstable per image. Twelve solid circles cannot represent edges, so
steering toward them sacrifices the flat-region coverage LPIPS also rewards.

\textbf{Control 3: the serialization (entropy coding).} A static corpus-trained
model over the fixed-width fields saves only $3$--$5\%$ cross-dataset ($<10\%$
even in-corpus); the residual-driven fitter already spreads positions
near-uniformly and uses the full color gamut, so the fields are near-maximum
entropy. There is no slack in the bitstream to recover.

\section{Discussion}
The three controls converge: neither a better MSE search, nor a perceptual
objective, nor entropy coding moves the perceptual frontier, and the Marwood
comparison shows an MSE-optimal mesh is perceptually \emph{worse} than coarse
hard-edged primitives. With the objective and the serialization ruled out, the
binding constraint in this regime is what the primitives can \emph{represent at
all}---their expressiveness. This unifies the paper's measurements: it is why
triangles, which carry oriented edges, beat circles perceptually throughout; why
the within-codec DCT control collapses to BlurHash territory; and why every
smoothing method (BlurHash, Gouraud meshes) holds PSNR while failing LPIPS. Through the rate--distortion--perception
lens~\cite{rdp}, this is the expected outcome: near the distortion floor
reachable at a few hundred bytes, further MSE optimization only trades perception
away. The actionable corollary for future work is to enrich the primitive vocabulary
(mixed-primitive selection, soft or differentiable edges), not to refine the
loss or the coder.

\textbf{Limitations.} The study instruments one open-source primitive codec; the
expressiveness argument should transfer, but other fitters may differ in
constants. We report two corpora (Kodak, CLIC); DIV2K is pending. The Marwood
comparison is our faithful reimplementation, not released code, though validated
to the paper's reported magnitude. SQIP appears only on the latency axis (its
rasterizer dependency was unavailable locally), so its perceptual point is
inferred from its $\sim$1\,kB SVG / $+3$\,dB-PSNR envelope rather than measured.
Finally, LPIPS and DISTS are learned proxies; a subjective study would further
harden the perceptual ranking.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented the first perceptual rate--distortion study of sub-300-byte image
placeholders, and used it to establish one counter-intuitive finding:
geometric-primitive hashing Pareto-dominates industrial formats and a faithful
Marwood~\cite{marwood} reimplementation on LPIPS and DISTS---by up to
$2.4$--$8\times$ in bytes---while a faithful, MSE-optimal, entropy-coded mesh
\emph{wins} PSNR yet loses perceptually. Three controls localize the regime's
bottleneck to primitive expressiveness, not the objective or the serialization.
We hope this establishes placeholders as a measurable research target rather than
folklore, and PSNR as the wrong yardstick for it.
