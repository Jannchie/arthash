Image placeholders - the low-fidelity previews shown while a full image loads -
are ubiquitous in production systems (BlurHash, ThumbHash, SQIP) yet have never
been evaluated in the academic literature, and the sub-300-byte regime they
occupy lies below where standard codecs operate. This is a measurement paper: we
present the first perceptual rate-distortion study of that regime, putting
industrial placeholder formats, a faithful reimplementation of the closest
academic prior (Marwood et al., ICIP 2018), and the smallest attainable
JPEG/WebP on common ground across Kodak and CLIC under PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, and
DISTS. The measurement yields one counter-intuitive finding. Geometric
primitives Pareto-dominate the perceptual frontier - a 20-byte circle hash beats
a 166-byte BlurHash on LPIPS - while every frequency/smoothing representation
collapses on it, including the MSE-optimal Marwood mesh, which wins PSNR yet
loses LPIPS 2.4x. In this regime PSNR is anti-correlated with perceptual quality.
Three controls (a quantization-aware search refinement, perceptual objective
weighting, and entropy coding) each fail to move the perceptual frontier, ruling
out the search objective and the serialization and localizing the binding
constraint to primitive expressiveness.
